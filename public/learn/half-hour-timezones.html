The World's Strangest Offsets: +05:45 and Friends

Chronosphere Editorial Team · Updated October 2026 · 4 min read

Most zones keep whole hours, but a stubborn club refuses:

  • Nepal +05:45 — the only mainstream :45 zone, set to Kathmandu's mean solar time (and a quiet assertion of identity).
  • India +05:30 and Sri Lanka +05:30 — the half-hour classic.
  • Myanmar +06:30, Afghanistan +04:30, Iran +03:30.
  • Newfoundland −03:30 (and −02:30 in DST), Marquesas −09:30.
  • Chatham Islands +12:45, Eucla (Australia) +08:45, Lord Howe +10:30.

    • Why?

    These places chose the mean solar time of their own territory — or a distinctive offset — rather than snapping to the nearest whole meridian. The result is a map of time that is proudly irregular.

    Software must not assume

    Offsets are pure data. Any code that assumes ":00" breaks on half the planet's interesting edges — which is exactly why Chronosphere reads the IANA tz database instead of guessing.
