The Terminator Line: Where Day Meets Night

Chronosphere Editorial Team · Updated October 2026 · 4 min read

The terminator is the moving boundary between daylight and darkness on Earth. At any instant it is (approximately) a great circle perpendicular to the Sun's direction.

It moves fast

Earth turns 40,075 km in 24 hours, so the terminator sweeps the equator at roughly 1,670 km/h.

It is not a knife edge

Atmospheric refraction and twilight — civil, nautical, astronomical — blur the line into a soft band, which is why sunsets take minutes, not instants.

Seasons tilt it

Earth's 23.44° axial tilt leans the terminator, producing midnight sun and polar night near the solstices.

How Chronosphere draws it

Each frame we compute the subsolar point (solar declination + subsolar longitude) and light the globe from exactly that direction. The shadow line you see is the real terminator for this exact second — not a texture.
